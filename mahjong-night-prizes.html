Game Night

Prizes worth bragging about.

A season with no stakes is just a bunch of hands. Keep the nightly prizes cheap and a little silly, and save anything with real weight for the end of the season.

Every session

Small stakes, every night

These should cost nothing or next to nothing. The fun is in the ritual, not the item.

Rotating Trophy

Pick one ridiculous object that lives with whoever won last. My table does this with a big wine glass: the winner drinks from it next time. A weird mug, a plastic crown, a stuffed animal nobody likes, all work. The only rule is it has to travel to whoever wins next.

Prize Bag

Nail polish, a mini candle, a lottery ticket, a good pen, or lean into the theme: koozies, shufflers, dice, beaded bracelets, card line finders, wine charms, transparent card sleeves, cocktail napkins, coasters, or print reusable cups, all mahjong themed. Keep three or four options and let the winner pick.

Collectibles

Every win earns a small pin or bracelet worn to the next session. Over a season, frequent winners end up wearing a little stack of them, visible without asking. The app already tracks every win, so nobody has to keep count by hand.

Privileges

Winner picks the music, skips setup duty next time, gets first pick of seat, or chooses the snack. Free, and it still feels like something.

End of season

The prizes with real weight

This is where it's worth spending a little. The app already tracks multiple winners, so pick prizes accordingly.

Grand Champion

Total points across the season. This is the season's headline, so give it a real prize: a bottle of something nice, a gift card, a piece of jewelry, or an engraved something if you want to go all in.

Runner-up & third

Smaller versions of the Grand Champion prize. Doesn't need to be a huge step down in thoughtfulness, just in price.

Hot Hand

Best average per session attended, not total, so it rewards whoever plays the best mahjong rather than whoever had the most free evenings. A player who could only make four nights out of ten can still win something real.

A just-for-fun last place prize

Optional, and only worth doing if your table's sense of humor can take it. A "wooden spoon," a joke certificate, a small gag gift, or a copy of Mastering American Mahjong by Megan Trottier (or any "how to play" guide), handed over with a wink. Frame it as affectionate, not a punishment, and skip it entirely if anyone would feel singled out.

Playing with kids

A shorter season, real stakes

My family runs a separate kids' season alongside the grown-up one. Two months instead of a full season, since that's about as long as a kid's attention holds up, with real money on the line.

How ours works

Every point a kid earns is worth $1 toward a gift card of their choice. Whoever wins the two-month season gets a $5 bonus added to theirs. Small enough to stay reasonable, real enough that they're counting their points.

A kid-sized Grand Champion

A paper crown, a certificate, or just letting them announce their own win at dinner. The app already tracks the standings, so there's no math for you to do at bedtime.

Pick the next family activity

Winner chooses the next movie night, the next takeout order, or the next weekend outing. Costs nothing extra on top of the gift card money, and it still feels like winning something.

A consolation prize for everyone else

A small treat for whoever didn't win keeps the season from feeling like a punishment for anyone. Ice cream after the last session works well.

A wall of fame poster

A poster taped up at home, not just a phone album. The winner's name and the date get added after every session, so by season's end it's a real thing they can point to, not just a line in the app.

Winner picks the next twist

Instead of an object, the winner chooses a house-rule change for next time: skip the Charleston, play messy mahjong (dump the tiles and shuffle by hand instead of building a wall), or try towers (trade whole blind stacks of tiles instead of passing chosen ones). Costs nothing and keeps the games from feeling identical.

A little more

Keep the whole season fun

A hall of fame

A corkboard, a fridge photo, or just a running album on someone's phone of every night's winner holding the rotating trophy object. Low effort, and it makes the season feel like it's building toward something.

Theme nights

Pick a color, a decade, a snack theme. Doesn't need a prize of its own, it's just another reason to look forward to a session.

A season-end certificate

Something simple with the season name, the Grand Champion, and the Hot Hand winner on it, printed or texted out at the closing session.
